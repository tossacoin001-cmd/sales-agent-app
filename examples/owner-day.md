# Fictional sample day — not customer proof

Aster Studio is a fictional two-person design business. Its owner wants ₦600,000 in account-verified new client receipts by month end.

Known evidence: a reconciled ₦180,000 deposit, an unpaid ₦200,000 invoice, a buyer asking about delivery dates and another opportunity without a confirmed approver.

The dashboard shows verified cash ₦180k, gap ₦420k and invoiced value ₦200k separately. It gives no win probability without comparable history.

## Action 1: Review the delivery question
The card links to the actual buyer request and unanswered thread. A draft uses only owner-approved capacity; it does not invent a start date. The owner edits and approves. Provider acceptance is recorded while delivery remains unconfirmed.

## Action 2: Request a written decision
The quote was already approved and the buyer agreed to a review date. Before queueing, check the latest conversation, opt-out status, quiet hours and prior follow-up. If a reply arrives first, the draft becomes stale and is not sent automatically.

## Action 3: Reconcile the payment
A provider shows a payment with settlement pending. The product displays it separately from account-verified receipts. After verified settlement, the ledger updates once even if the webhook repeats.

At close, the owner completed two actions and one payment remains unverified. The app reports actual work and the remaining gap. It does not add an invoice to income or claim its draft generated a pre-existing deposit.

The value hypothesis is less time finding the next step and fewer unanswered decisions. Neither is a guaranteed revenue increase.
